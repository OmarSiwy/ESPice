# ParEval on real hardware — the wall-clock numbers

Closes item 12 of `remaining-2026-09-10.md` ("ParEval has never been benchmarked
on real hardware"). Wall clock only; the Ir figure quoted there is unusable and
was not re-taken.

## Verdict

**ParEval never wins.** Across six decks and six widths, the best wall clock
ever recorded at any lane count is **1.07×** (`parallel_inverters_2000`, 2
lanes, one sweep out of three — the other two put the same point at 0.90× and
0.96×). Every deck loses at 4 lanes and above. On
`parallel_inverters_2000` the answer to the headline question is
**0.78–0.92× at 8, 0.67–0.76× at 16, 0.60–0.74× at 32** — all slowdowns.

It is also expensive to lose: `rc_ladder_100k` at 32 lanes burns **137 s of CPU
for a 9.2 s wall clock** against 4.5 s CPU / 4.5 s wall serial. A 30× energy
multiplier for a 2× slowdown.

Recommendation: **leave it opt-in and leave `default_min_instances` alone.** No
code change ships with this document. Three concrete defects found by reading
the code during the investigation are listed at the end; none of them is worth
fixing until a deck exists whose parallel fraction justifies threading at all.

## The rig, and the contention it ran under

- i9-14900HX, **hybrid**: CPUs 0–15 are 8 P-cores with SMT (5.6–5.8 GHz), CPUs
  16–31 are 16 single-threaded E-cores (4.1 GHz). "32 cores" is 24 physical
  cores of two different speeds, which matters for reading any curve past 8.
- Binary: main tree `zig-out/bin/espice`, 2026-09-10 13:35, `446268a` plus a
  6-line `bsim4va.va` delta that no deck here touches. Invocation is the
  benchmark runner's: `espice -b --backend cpu -r <raw> <deck>`.
- **The box was shared for the whole session.** Load average ranged from 5.6 to
  29 (other agents running `zig`/`clang` builds, a `callgrind`, an `ngspice`, a
  `quasistatic` job, Spotify). `parallel_inverters_2000` serial is quoted at
  899 ms in the handoff doc; the fastest serial run observed here was 1109 ms
  and serial medians ranged 1300–1833 ms depending on the hour.
- Mitigation: every sweep is **round-robin over widths**, so drift hits all arms
  equally; 5–21 repeats per cell; median, min, max and spread all reported.
  Three independent `parallel_inverters_2000` sweeps were taken hours apart
  under load 6, 20 and 24, and they agree on every conclusion below. Where a
  reading looked anomalous it is called out inline.
- Harness: `/tmp/pv/bench.py` (round-robin, `perf_counter` wall,
  `RUSAGE_CHILDREN` CPU, md5 per raw), `/tmp/pv/rawdiff.py` (binary raw
  comparator). Not committed — one `subprocess` loop and one `struct.unpack`.

Spread is large (17–260%) and that is contention, not measurement error: the
minimum column tracks the median in every arm, and the sign of every result is
the same on medians and on minima.

## The gate — exact predicate

`src/engine.zig:296-311`, run once per `Simulation.fromNetlist`:

```
io != null
  && getenv("ESPICE_THREADS") parses as u32
  && lanes >= 2
  && sum(batch.count for batch in circuit.batches) >= devices.par.default_min_instances   // 1024
  && ParEval.init(..., @min(lanes, 16)) succeeds
```

Any failure `break`s to `par_eval = null`, silently. `default_min_instances` is
`src/devices/engine.zig:2304`.

**`@min(lanes, 16)` is a hard clamp nobody documented.** `ESPICE_THREADS=32` runs
16 lanes. Confirmed empirically, not just read: the raw output at 32 is
byte-identical to 16 on all six decks, and the timings are within noise
(`parallel_inverters_2000` 2452.7 vs 2483.6 ms; `resistor_grid` 155.5 vs
164.5). Every "32" row in this document is really 16 lanes plus the cost of
allocating for 16 again.

## Scaling

Speedup and efficiency are against `ESPICE_THREADS=1`, which does **not**
construct a `ParEval` at all (`lanes < 2` fails the gate) — it is the true
serial path, not a one-lane ParEval.

## Correctness at every width

Three repeats per (deck, width), md5 of the raw file, plus a full-precision
word-by-word comparison against `ESPICE_THREADS=1`.

**Run-to-run determinism holds at every width on every deck** — one md5 per
cell, 18 cells, 54 runs. The handoff doc's 4-lane claim extends to 8, 16 and 32
unchanged. `T=16` and `T=32` produce the identical file everywhere, which is the
`@min(lanes,16)` clamp showing through.

| deck | vs serial |
|---|---|
| `rc_ladder_10k` | **bit-identical at 2/4/8/16/32** |
| `rc_ladder_100k` | **bit-identical at 2/4/8/16/32** |
| `resistor_grid_100x100` | 163–1252 of 10002 words differ, `max_abs` 4.441e-16, `max_rel` 2.217e-16 at every width |
| `parallel_inverters_500` | 226→596 words of 300980; `max_abs` 1.4e-17 (T=2) → 1.0e-15 (T=16/32) |
| `parallel_inverters_2000` | 163→596 words of 1194980; `max_abs` 1.4e-17 (T=2) → 2.0e-14 (T=16/32) |
| `opamp_wl_5000` | 91 words of 2731638 at T=2/4/8; 602 at T=16/32; `max_abs` 1.5e-12 |

Every deviation is last-ulp reassociation, as documented. The two linear ladders
are bit-identical because each stage's stamps land wholly inside one lane.

**One number needs explaining and is not a bug.** `opamp_wl_5000` at T=16/32
reports `max_rel` **1.831** — a 183% relative deviation. It is
`v(out_888)` real part at AC point 73: `-5.978e-13` becomes `+4.969e-13`. The
complex magnitude of that same variable at that point is `1.629e-07`, and the
largest magnitude anywhere in that column is `1.995e+07`. The real part is
`3.7e-6` of its own phasor and `3e-20` of the file scale; it is a fully
cancelled component and the relative metric is meaningless on it. `max_abs`
stays at 1.5e-12.

Worth recording, though: at 16 lanes the deviation set stops being "everything a
little" and becomes **exactly the four nodes of OTA instance #888**
(`out_888`, `d1_888`, `tail_888`, plus `i(vdd)`), at 8 lanes it is `i(vdd)`
alone. The reason is that the doc's guarantee — `init` cuts lane ranges at
instance boundaries — protects a single instance's own cancelling `+g/-g` pair
but does **not** protect a node shared by two instances in the same batch.
`tail_888` is driven by `M1_888`, `M2_888` and `M5_888`, all in the nch batch;
a lane cut between them splits that node's row sum. Bounded, deterministic, and
1 ulp, but the invariant as written in `engine.zig:2266-2290` is stronger than
what the code delivers. Worth a one-line correction to that comment.

## Where it stops scaling, and why

Four controls, each of which rules something out.

**1. It is not core placement, SMT or the E-cores.** Pinned to eight *distinct*
P-cores with no SMT sibling sharing (`taskset -c 0,2,4,6,8,10,12,14`),
`parallel_inverters_2000` still loses: 1356.2 ms at T=1 against 1575.9 / 1660.1 /
1647.1 at T=2/4/8 — 0.86, 0.82, 0.82. Perfect placement does not save it.

**2. The work does parallelize; the fixed cost is what kills it.** Pinned to the
16 E-cores, where a lane's share of work is 2.1× larger relative to a fixed
overhead, the same deck **speeds up**: 2794.2 ms at T=1 against 2273.6 / 2188.1 /
2162.4 at T=2/4/8 — **1.23, 1.28, 1.29×**. Same code, same deck, same lane count,
slower cores, and the sign flips. That is the cleanest evidence in this document
that ParEval's problem is a fixed per-eval cost, not a broken partition or a
broken kernel. (T=16 on 16 E-cores collapses to 0.70× — full subscription plus
the master thread.)

**3. The overhead scales with matrix size, not with fork/join count.**
`rc_ladder_10k` and `rc_ladder_100k` are the same circuit at 10× the size: same
`.tran 1n 200n`, both 236 accepted points, both linear, so the number of
fork/joins is the same and only n and nnz change.

| | 10k | 100k | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| n | 10003 | 100003 | 10.0× |
| T=1 median | 356.3 ms | 4510.2 ms | 12.7× |
| T=8 median − T=1 median | +346.1 ms | +3311.3 ms | **9.6×** |

The added cost tracks n almost exactly. It is the per-eval plane traffic — the
master's `@memcpy(g_vals, g_base)` and clears, each lane zeroing its window, and
`reduce` reading every lane's slab back and adding it in — not the thread
handoff, which is per-fork/join and would have been flat.

Peak RSS confirms the footprint directly (`/usr/bin/time -f %M`):

| deck | T=1 | T=8 | T=16 | Δ per extra lane |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `rc_ladder_100k` | 148.4 MB | 192.0 MB | 241.7 MB | 6.2 MB |
| `rc_ladder_10k` | 18.7 MB | 23.2 MB | 29.9 MB | 0.74 MB |
| `parallel_inverters_2000` | 26.1 MB | 29.0 MB | 32.8 MB | 0.45 MB |

6.2 MB per lane × 15 extra lanes is 93 MB of private slab against a 36 MB L3.
`rc_ladder_100k` at 16 lanes cannot keep its reduction working set in cache at
all, which is why that deck is the worst in the table.

**4. Above 8 lanes the yield loop thrashes.** `/usr/bin/time -f "%S %c"` on
`parallel_inverters_2000` pinned to CPUs 0–15:

| T | sys (s) | involuntary ctx switches |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.03 | 4 |
| 2 | 0.19 | 374 |
| 4 | 0.72 | 52 |
| 8 | 2.06 | 479 |
| 16 | 19.65 | **1,041,937** |

That is `forkJoin`/`workerMain` spinning 4096 times and then calling
`std.Thread.yield()` (`src/devices/engine.zig:2537, 2559`). At 16 lanes on 16
hardware threads, with a loaded box, a yielded worker is not rescheduled
promptly and the master waits at the barrier for it. This is the cliff at
T=16 in every deck's row.

**And the ceiling is low even if all of that were free.** Amdahl, solved
backwards from the best measured points: `parallel_inverters_2000` at 1.071× on
2 lanes implies a parallel fraction f ≈ 0.13; `opamp_wl_5000` at 1.20× on 4
lanes implies f ≈ 0.22. Those give infinite-lane ceilings of **1.15× and
1.29×**. Only device evaluation is laned — LU factor, triangular solve,
`stepBound`, `updateStates`, the timestep controller and the 9.6 MB of waveform
output are all serial. The handoff doc's Ir budget (device kernel + stamp
54.7% of `parallel_inverters_100`) does not carry over to wall clock, because
the LU on n=2005 is cache-miss bound and costs far more time per instruction
than the kernel does.

One extra cost the parallel path pays that serial does not, found by reading
`src/analysis/Circuit.zig:335-341`: **`evalQ` degrades to a full `eval` whenever
`par_eval != null`.** The transient's post-accept charge re-read is charge-only
serially; under ParEval it becomes a complete device pass with all four planes
cleared and restamped, once per accepted timestep. At
`parallel_inverters_2000`'s 596 accepted points against roughly 1350 Newton
evaluations for this topology, that is **+40–45% device-eval passes**, paid only
when threading is on. It is consistent with the shape of the results: the two
decks that are not transient (`resistor_grid_100x100` `.op`, `opamp_wl_5000`
`.ac`) are the only two that stay near 1.0×, and every transient deck loses.
The magnitude was not isolated separately — that needs a build.

## The two proposals in `arpice-devices.md` — measured, both no-go

**"Add processor hints to ParEval's short spin waits"** (that doc, line 105).
The premise — "Neither polling loop calls `std.atomic.spinLoopHint`" — is
**stale**. Both loops call it today, `forkJoin` at `engine.zig:2537` and
`workerMain` at `engine.zig:2559`, with the comment explaining why. Nothing to
do. The real spin problem is the `sched_yield` after 4096 iterations (control 4
above), and a hint does not touch it.

**"Balance CPU partitions using device evaluation cost"** (line 77). Not the
bottleneck, and its own text says why: "expected to do little for homogeneous
batches". Every deck measured here is homogeneous — 4000 identical MOS1 in
`parallel_inverters_2000`, 100000 identical R and C in `rc_ladder_100k`, 25000
MOS1 in three sizes in `opamp_wl_5000`. The existing `count * n_u * n_u` weight
already balances all of them, and imbalance cannot explain a deck where every
instance is bit-identical work. Control 2 (the E-core speedup) independently
rules it out: the same partition scales fine when the work per lane is large
enough.

**"Tile the ordered CPU lane reduction"** (line 67) is the one whose premise the
measurements *support* — control 3 shows reduction/clear traffic is the cost
that scales, and the RSS table shows the slabs overflow L3 on
`rc_ladder_100k`. It is still not worth building: tiling the reduce to zero cost
cannot lift `parallel_inverters_2000` above its 1.15× Amdahl ceiling, and
`rc_ladder_100k` is a linear deck whose device evaluation should not be threaded
in the first place. Per the brief, not implemented.

## Recommendation on the default

**Keep ParEval opt-in. Do not lower, raise or otherwise touch
`default_min_instances`.**

The brief asked for a defensible threshold measured at the crossover. There is
no crossover to measure: instance count is not the variable that decides whether
threading pays. `parallel_inverters_2000` has 6002 instances — 5.9× the current
gate — and loses at every width. `rc_ladder_100k` has 200001 instances, 195× the
gate, and is the worst deck in the table at 0.49×. The variable that decides is
the *parallel fraction of wall clock*, which is 13–22% on this corpus, and no
instance-count threshold can observe it. Raising the gate would not rescue any
deck; lowering it would only expose more of them.

The gate as written is therefore correct by accident and should stay. What is
worth changing is the honesty of the surrounding code, in three places, none of
which is a performance change:

1. **`@min(lanes, 16)` at `src/engine.zig:310` is undocumented and silent.**
   `ESPICE_THREADS=32` on a 32-thread box runs 16 lanes. Either say so in the
   comment or take it from `std.Thread.getCpuCount()`.
2. **`evalQ` → full `eval` at `src/analysis/Circuit.zig:335-341`** makes the
   threaded transient path do 40–45% more device evaluations than the serial
   one. The existing `ponytail:` note there already proposes the fix (a
   `.charge` `Mode` for ParEval). It should be a prerequisite for any future
   attempt, not a footnote.
3. **The instance-boundary invariant at `src/devices/engine.zig:2266-2290`**
   claims more than it delivers. It prevents one instance's cancelling pair from
   straddling a lane; it does not prevent two instances in the same batch that
   share a node from doing so, which is what `opamp_wl_5000`'s OTA #888 shows at
   16 lanes. Still 1 ulp, still deterministic — but the comment should say
   "instance", not imply "node".

If someone wants to revisit this, the target that would actually move is a deck
whose parallel fraction exceeds ~0.6. Nothing in the fixture corpus is close,
and item 3 of `remaining-2026-09-10.md` (the dead dedup cache) would *reduce*
the parallel fraction further on exactly the deck — `parallel_inverters_2000` —
where threading looks most attractive.

Nothing. Documentation only; `zig build test` is untouched at its 305/307
