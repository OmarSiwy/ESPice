# Limiting measured AGAINST ngspice

2026-09-10. `docs/perf/remaining-2026-09-10.md` §2 says `D.limit`'s body is
**291 Ir per instance per Newton iterate against ngspice's 52**, on a pass worth
14.4% of `scaling/parallel_inverters_100` and 10.6% of `devices/mos6_inverter`.
This is the itemisation of that 5.6×, and what came off it.

Method follows `docs/device-eval-vs-ngspice-2026-09.md`: measure both
implementations on the same data, in the same rig, and split by line.

---

## The rig

The in-tree 291 and ngspice's 52 come from different denominators — one is an
ablation inside a whole transient, the other a hand count. So both were rebuilt
side by side:

* `/tmp/limrig/rig.zig` — the `engine.zig limitRange` walk verbatim (gath tape →
  `cur`/`old` → `D.limit` → `lim_x`), importing the REAL generated
  `mos1.device.zig` and `VerA/tools/contract.zig`. No `Dual` anywhere, so
  measurement hazard 1 (a rig's `dual.zig` drifting from `DualFor`) cannot
  apply — the limit pass is pure f64.
* `/tmp/limrig/ng.c` — `DEVfetlim`/`DEVpnjlim`/`DEVlimvds` copied verbatim from
  `ngspice-44.2/src/spicelib/devices/devsup.c`, driven by the MOS1 ladder from
  `mos1load.c:351-383` with `CKTfixLimit == 0`. `zig cc -O2 -march=native`.

Both run 256 instances × 4000 iterates over the SAME node array: 104 nodes
(a real inverter chain has ~105 for 200 MOSFETs, so `x` is L1-resident and the
indices are scattered), per-role rail/mid-swing/reverse-bias voltages, ±1 mV
jitter, and a per-iterate perturbation so `lim_x` is a live dependence. Ir is
`valgrind --tool=callgrind`, per-line via `callgrind_annotate --auto=yes`. The
rig binaries carry symbols, which the espice binary cannot
(`-Ddebug-info=true` SEGVs the compiler).

Both rigs live in `/tmp`, which this machine wipes (hazard 4). They are
described here rather than committed because each is under 200 lines and is
reconstructed from this section plus the two files it copies — a rig in-tree
would be scaffolding that drifts from the code it measures.

Every figure below is **Ir per MOSFET per Newton iterate**.

| | espice | ngspice 44.2 | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| whole limiting pass | 174.1 | 71.1 | 2.45× |
| — the walk around the body | 48.1 | 15 | |
| — the body (`D.limit` / the ladder) | 126.0 | 56 | 2.25× |

ngspice's 15 is its own gather (four branch voltages out of node voltages, 10)
plus its `CKTstate0` write-back (five doubles, 5) — and in the real simulator
the gather is FREE, because `mos1load` needed `vgs`/`vgd`/`vbs` for the model
evaluation anyway. Its 56 splits as `DEVfetlim` 15.9 + `DEVpnjlim` 13.0 +
`DEVlimvds` 4.0 = 32.9 of kernel, 15.1 of mode scaffolding (two branches, three
derivations by subtraction, `Check`) and 8.0 of register traffic callgrind
cannot attribute to a line. So the handoff's "52" is this 56 to within how the
scaffolding is apportioned, and the comparison stands.

## Where our 126 went, and what is not in ngspice's 56

Per-line, all transparent paths taken:

| line | Ir | in ngspice? |
|---|---:|---|
| `sel()` — `av`/`bv` bitcasts, the asm barrier, the mask-or | **22** | no |
| `zPnjlim` transparent arm ×2 — damp guard 6, `floor` 8, `floored` 5 | 19 | 13, once |
| `zFetlim` transparent arm — 13 in the callee, 7 of call + spill at the site | **20** | 15.9, inlined |
| `zLimvds` transparent arm | 8 | 4.0 |
| mode ladder — `vdso`, `sgt`, mode branch, two probes, two write-backs, `dn`/`dl` | 21 | ~15 |
| pnjlim site scaffolding ×2 — probes, `sg`, `sg *`, write-back, `ok` | 22 | ~4 |
| `return .{ .x = x, .converged = ok }` | 2 | — |
| spill/reload with no line attribution | **29** | ~8 |

And the walk's 48.1 is 8 `gath` u32 loads + 8 indexed `x` loads + 8 `lim_x`
loads + 8 `lim_x` stores, for a ladder that reads four unknowns and writes two.

### The excess, named

1. **The `[n_u]f64` ABI was walked at full width.** `limit`'s signature has to
   be `[n_u]f64` twice in and once out — a host cannot name a device's
   unknowns — but mos1's ladder reads `{g, b, di, si}` and writes `{di, si}`.
   `d`, `s` and the two branch-flow unknowns were gathered from `x`, copied
   through the frame and written back into `lim_x` **to arrive at the value
   they already had**, once per instance per iterate. ngspice has no such
   traffic at all: its limiter memory is the three branch voltages in
   `CKTstate0`, not every node the device touches. ≈24 Ir.

2. **The kernels were one function each, with a fast path bolted on.**
   `-femit-asm` on the rig: `zFetlim` was big enough that LLVM **refused to
   inline it** — a real `call mos1.device.zFetlim` with six caller-saved spills
   around it, 20 Ir for a transparent arm worth ten. `zPnjlim` and `zLimvds`
   went the other way and inlined WHOLE, branchless ladder included, into the
   caller's register pressure — which is most of the 29 Ir of unattributed
   spill. clang inlines all three `DEV*` because each is small. ≈26 Ir.

3. **`sel`'s asm barrier was being paid on the transparent path.** The barrier
   exists so LLVM cannot branch-lower the CLAMP ladder's selects; `zPnjlim`
   computed `floor` and `floored` with it *before* its early return, so every
   transparent iterate paid four GPR round trips for two picks a predictor gets
   right — on a path that is already behind a branch. Part of the 26.

4. **The frame sign was recomputed at every clamp.** `const sg: f64 = if
   (inst.lp__1 < 0) -1.0 else 1.0` four times per body, over a value
   `precompute` latched. ngspice folds the sign into the stored state
   (`type*vbe`) and never recovers it. ≈5 Ir.

5. **NOT an excess, though the handoff listed it as a candidate: "a branchy
   ladder where ngspice has a straight-line one".** The opposite is true.
   ngspice's ladder is *more* branchy — a mode branch, a vds-sign branch, and
   four regions inside `DEVfetlim` — and it is fast because those branches are
   predictable and the bodies are small. Our transparent tests are the same
   shape.

6. **Left on the table, deliberately: we run TWO `pnjlim` calls where ngspice
   runs one.** `mos1load.c` limits `vbs` OR `vbd` by the sign of `vds` and
   derives the other by subtraction; `mos1.va` declares two independent
   `$limit(V(b,si))` / `$limit(V(b,di))` calls and we honour both. That is ≈22
   Ir, and taking it would change **which iterates Newton accepts** — a
   convergence change wearing an optimisation's clothes. Not touched.

## What changed

Two commits, one on each side, useless apart: VerA emits the masks, ARPice
reads them.

* **VerA `limit-body-vera` 248c0c6** — `limit_kernels.zig`: every kernel is now
  an `inline fn` holding only the §9.17.3 transparent test, tail-calling a
  `noinline` `*Clamp` that is the unchanged branchless ladder; `zPnjlim`'s
  transparent arm trades its two `sel`s for branches. `cg_limit.zig`: one
  `const zsg__k: f64 = ±1.0` per DISTINCT frame sign at the top of `limit`,
  plus `limit_reads` / `limit_writes` as u64 masks over `U`, computed by
  mirroring the emitter site for site. `contract.zig`: `limitReads` /
  `limitWrites` accessors that default to ALL, so a device without the decls
  behaves exactly as before.
* **ARPice `limit-body`** — `limitRange` gathers `cur`/`old` on `limit_reads`
  and scatters on `limit_writes`; `evalRange` reads the limited plane and
  builds `corr` on `limit_writes` only (the other lanes are a comptime zero, so
  both reductions shrink); `seedFn` and the GPU `limitKernel` follow the same
  masks; `lim_x` is zeroed at allocation because gpu_context uploads the whole
  plane.

`lim_x` keeps its `count * n_u` stride, so the scatter-tape ABI and
`layout_hash` do not move.

Rig ladder, each step bit-identical (same `chk` sum over the limited plane):

| | Ir/inst | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|
| baseline | 174.0 | |
| + kernels split hot/cold | 148.0 | −26 |
| + hoisted frame sign | 143.0 | −5 |
| + masked host walk | **119.0** | −24 |
| ngspice | 71.1 | |

2.45× → 1.68×. Of the residual 48, item 6 above is 22.

### The one place this is not bit-neutral, stated rather than buried

An unknown the device READS but never WRITES (mos1's `g` and `b`) now takes its
previous value from `x_old` instead of from the copy `lim_x` used to carry.
Those are the same number wherever `postStep` runs once per `x_old` update,
which is every CPU path: `finalizeStep` latches `x_old = x` immediately before
`x += dx`, so the `x` a limit pass saw last time IS the `x_old` it is handed
this time. `newton_core`'s monotone-residual retreat calls `postStep` a SECOND
time against the same `x_old`, and there the two differ — that is
`backtrack = true`, which both CPU envs set false (`converger.zig:351`,
`newton_core.zig:417`) and only a GPU solve turns on.

## Result

`zig build -Doptimize=ReleaseFast`, `valgrind --tool=callgrind`, NR iteration
counts from `ZP_TRAN_STATS=1`. Baseline is ARPice 446268a + VerA a19b6fb.

| deck | Ir before | Ir after | Δ | NR iters before → after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `scaling/parallel_inverters_100` | 466,358,459 | 433,940,840 | **−6.95%** | 1352 → **1352** |
| `devices/mos6_inverter` | 153,736,881 | 146,039,892 | **−5.01%** | 884 → **884** |

Attempts and accepted points are also unchanged (pi100 613/595, mos6 320/316),
which they must be, because the output is bit-identical.

**Bit-identity: 248 of 248 fixture raws byte-identical, 0 differ, and the exit
code of every one of the 280 decks in `benchmark/fixtures` matches.** (The 32
decks with no raw are `UnsupportedDevice` skips, and they skip identically.)

pi100's saving is **119.9 Ir per MOSFET per Newton iterate** — 32,417,619 over
1352 × 200. The rig predicted 55. The real deck saves more than double because
its iterates are NOT all transparent: an inverter chain in mid-transition takes
`zPnjlim`'s damping arm (three `@log` libcalls) and the fetlim/limvds clamp
ladders on a real fraction of iterates, and those used to be inlined into the
hot body's register allocation. Outlining them helps the slow path as well as
the fast one. That also explains the gap between the rig's 126-Ir body and the
handoff's 291: the handoff measured pi100's real bias mix, the rig measures the
transparent path alone.

`zig build test --summary all`: 259/259 steps, **406/406 tests**. (One earlier
run tripped the stderr gate `remaining-2026-09-10.md` §13 documents — the
device binary prints two model-catalog warnings and the aggregate step reads
stderr as failure. It passes 11/11 with exit 0 standalone and passed cleanly on
the re-run.)

## What is still there

* The second `pnjlim` (item 6) — ≈22 Ir, and a convergence change.
* `var x = cur` still copies the read lanes, and each clamp writes back through
  `x[@intFromEnum(U.si)] -= vl - vn` on an array where ngspice assigns a
  register. Closing that means `limit` returning a sparse result rather than
  `[n_u]f64`, i.e. a contract change; the masks are the cheap 80% of it.
* `zPnjlim`'s transparent arm still computes its floor for every reverse-biased
  junction (5 Ir × 2), which ngspice also does. Not an excess.
