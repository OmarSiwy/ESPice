# The Dual AD layer, itemised — and where mos6 is actually behind ngspice

2026-09-10, branch `dual-ad`. Scope: `docs/perf/remaining-2026-09-10.md` §1, the
device kernel. Two questions were open — *what are the 583 Ir of `Dual` methods
spending on*, and *which blocks are we behind ngspice on* — and both are now
answered with measurements rather than estimates.

**Result: a NO-GO on the AD layer, with the ledger that earns it.** Every
`Dual` primitive is already at or below its naive AVX2 lowering; LLVM folds
37% of the nominal derivative arithmetic away before it is emitted. What is
left is the width, and every width reduction is in the settled table. The
measured delta on both gate decks is therefore **0** — no hot-path code
changed. Three side results did fall out and are recorded below: doc item 6
(`updateStates`) is **fixed**, lead 3 (`pcExpensive`) is **closed**, and lead 2
(`sel`) has a ceiling that is smaller than the doc's estimate.

---

## The rig

`/tmp/rig` (`measure.sh <tag> [mos6|mos1]`). Three deliberate choices, each
answering a hazard from the handoff doc:

- **The dual is the real one.** `measure.sh` *copies* `src/devices/engine.zig`
  and `sed`s one word (`fn DualFor` → `pub fn DualFor`) so the rig can name the
  collapsed instantiation. There is no hand-written `dual.zig` to drift, and
  `gompute.math` comes along by construction. Hazard 1 cannot recur.
- **The device is the one the build produced**, copied out of `.zig-cache/o`.
- **The consumption is `evalRange`'s.** Every residual row is read and the
  derivative lanes are read exactly where `jac_pattern`/`q_pattern` declare
  them. Reading one lane lets LLVM scalarize the dual (hazard 3); reading all
  `2·n_u²` invents rig-only work.

Model/instance cards are the deck's `N10L5` (mos6) and `N10L1` (mos1)
verbatim. **No `AD/AS/PD/PS`** — the deck gives no areas, so `Cbs == Cbssw == 0`
and the junction-charge block is dead, exactly as in ngspice (hazard 2). Bias is
a saturated n-channel (`vd=2.5 vg=5 vs=vb=0`), which is the **expensive** arm:
ngspice's own average over this deck is 2.39 `exp` and 0.51 `log` per
instance-eval because ~49% of evaluations are in cutoff.

Sanity: the rig reproduces **mos1 = 755 Ir/eval** against the handoff doc's 754.

Two ancillary rigs: `countrig.zig` wraps `Dual` in a counting adapter (exact
dynamic S-op census, branch directions unchanged); `urig.zig` drives
`updateState` and the `RealFor` charge-only pass.

## 1. Where the AD cost goes, itemised

mos6 `evalQ`, saturated bias, **999 Ir/eval**. Split by source file, from
`callgrind_annotate --auto=yes` on a `-fno-strip` build (n = 2000, per-eval =
Ir/2000):

| | Ir/eval | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| `engine.zig` — **the Dual AD layer** | **488** | **48.8%** |
| `gompute/device/math.zig` — transcendentals | 230 | 23.0% |
| generated `mos6.zig` — model expressions | 176 | 17.6% |
| `rig.zig` — seed + pattern-gated lane reads (`evalRange`'s two ends) | 105 | 10.5% |

Same shape as the doc's "583 / 309 / 264 / 96 at 1403", one prefix-latch and
one shared-`ln` later. An independent check: zeroing **every** arithmetic
derivative in `Dual` (values untouched, so every branch takes the same
direction) gives **541 Ir/eval**, i.e. the AD layer is **458 Ir** by ablation
against 488 by line attribution. The two methods agree to 6%.

### The 488, by method

Per-line Ir from the same annotation, against the exact dynamic call census.
`splat` and `mulAddV` are inline helpers; their cost is attributed to their own
lines, not to the caller's.

| `Dual` line | Ir/eval | executed calls | Ir/call | naive AVX2 lowering |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `scale` `.d = a.d * splat(c)` | 56 | 31 | 1.81 | 3 (+splat) |
| `add` | 49 | 20 | 2.45 | 3 |
| `sub` | 48 | 30 | 1.60 | 3 |
| `splat` (helper) | 35 | ~69 sites | 0.51 | 1 |
| `mul` | 28 | 12 | 2.33 | 5 (+2 splat) |
| `mulAddV` (helper) | 27 | 17 | 1.59 | 2 |
| `addC` (no derivative work at all) | 15 | 21 | 0.71 | 1 |
| `max` | 15 | 7 | 2.14 | 2 |
| `div` (all three lines) | 10 | 5 | 2.00 | 6 (+2 splat) |
| `sqrt` | 7 | 1 | 7.0 | 7 |
| `exp` | 5 | 3 | 1.67 | 3 (+splat) |
| `min` | 5 | 2 | 2.50 | 2 |
| `neg` | 4 | 5 | 0.80 | 3 |
| `sel` | 2 | 2 | 1.00 | 1 |
| `con`, `val`, `lt`, `log` | 0 | 119 / 54 / 2 / 3 | 0 | — |
| **attributed** | **306** | | | |
| **unattributed lines in `engine.zig`** | **182** | | | |

**Two facts kill the "the AD layer is untouched, so there must be fat in it"
prior.**

1. **Every primitive is at or below its naive lowering.** Summing the naive
   column against the census gives **488 instructions** of nominal derivative
   arithmetic; the attributed measurement is **306**. LLVM is already deleting
   37% of it — mostly by propagating the structural zero out of `con()` and out
   of the `e_a − e_b` constant that a terminal-voltage difference produces. The
   "redundant gradient work on values whose gradient is provably zero" the
   handoff doc asks about **is already gone**, and it is gone for the same
   reason `solve-independent re-expansion` measured zero: GVN plus constant
   folding inside an `always_inline` body.
2. **The remaining 182 Ir (37% of the AD layer, 18% of the kernel) is
   allocator traffic, not arithmetic.** It attributes to no source line, and
   the static histogram of `main` is 386 `vmovapd` + 479 `vmovsd` against 184
   `vmulpd` + 62 `vaddpd`. `Dual` is 72 bytes — one f64 plus two ymm — and
   `evalQ` has 16 of them live at its return. That is the register-pressure tax
   the earlier audit already priced: outlining it is **+63% worse**, packing the
   structurally-zero rows is **−5, wrong sign**, and out-params are **exactly
   zero**.

### Per-class ablations, for the record

Each replaces one class's derivative with the cheapest form that still reads
both inputs (`cheap-`, an honest per-class price) or with a zero (`zero-`,
which cascades downstream and therefore over-counts).

| variant | Ir/eval | Δ | per call |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| base | 999 | — | |
| `cheap-mul` (`d = a.d + b.d`) | 947 | −52 | 4.3 × 12 |
| `cheap-div` | 969 | −30 | 6.0 × 5 |
| `cheap-scale` (`d = a.d`) | 892 | −107 | 3.5 × 31 |
| `cheap-exp+log+sqrt` | 986 | −13 | |
| `zero-all` (the `RealFor` bound) | 541 | **−458** | |
| `zero-mul` / `zero-div` / `zero-scale` | 690 / 868 / 599 | (cascades) | |

`cheap-add+sub+neg` is not measurable this way: making `add`'s derivative a
passthrough turns the whole chain back into the compile-time seed basis and
collapses to the `zero-all` number (548 vs 541).

### What is NOT there

- **No dead libm on the hot path.** The core computes `vcrits`/`vcritd`
  (`vt·ln(vt/√2·isat)`, generated `h[14]`/`h[16]`) on **every** entry, outside
  the `hp_ok` prefix region, from inputs that are all latched constants. It
  looks like two free `log` calls. It is not: LLVM dead-codes both in `evalQ`,
  in `updateState` and in the `RealFor` charge pass — replacing them with a
  constant measured **−2 / 0 / 0 Ir**. `precompute` already latches them into
  `lp__2`/`lp__4`, and `limit()` reads those fields rather than re-entering the
  core. Nothing to collect.
- **No transcendental redundancy.** 3 `exp` + 1 `log` + 1 `sqrt` execute per
  saturated eval; ngspice does 3 `exp` + 1 `log` + 1 `sqrt` on the same arm.
  542e9a0's shared `ln` put us exactly on ngspice's profile.
- **No globally dead lane.** The union of `jac_pattern` ∪ `q_pattern` over all
  rows is `0xff` — every one of the 8 lanes is read by some consumer. The
  "gradient lanes kept live that the consumer never reads" hypothesis is empty
  for mos6, which also re-confirms the reduced-basis no-go from the other end.

## 2. Block-by-block against ngspice 44.2

ngspice rebuilt from source **with symbols** (the `/tmp` wipe took the previous
one): `./configure --enable-xspice --enable-cider --disable-openmp
--with-readline=no --prefix=/tmp/ng/inst CFLAGS="-g -O2"`. Source read at
`/tmp/ng/ngspice-44.2/src/spicelib/devices/mos6/mos6load.c`. Both engines under
`valgrind --tool=callgrind --cache-sim=no --branch-sim=no`.

**Iteration counts are at parity, so every number below is per-evaluation
cost.** `devices/mos6_inverter` is 80 MOS6 instances: ngspice does **879**
`MOS6load` calls (70,320 instance-evals), espice **884** NR iterations.
`scaling/parallel_inverters_100`: ngspice 1319 `MOS1load` calls, espice 1352.

| deck | espice | ngspice 44.2 (from source) | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `scaling/parallel_inverters_100` | 466,364,568 | 513,471,898 | **0.908** |
| `devices/mos6_inverter` | 153,743,288 | 162,473,131 | **0.946** |

ngspice's whole per-instance MOS6 evaluation, from `MOS6load`'s **inclusive**
cost 83,947,749 ÷ 70,320 = **1,193.8 Ir**, decomposed by call site:

| ngspice block | calls/eval | Ir/eval |
|---|---:|---:|
| `MOS6load` self — preamble, bias recovery, bypass test, physics, Meyer glue, **26 stamps** | 1 | 800.7 |
| `NIintegrate` ×5 (2 junction + 3 Meyer) | 4.85 | 140.6 |
| channel `exp` ×2 (`vdsat`, `idsat`) | 1.02 | 64.2 |
| diode `exp` (b–s, b–d) | 1.37 | 70.2 |
| channel `log` (`sarg1 = log(vgon)`) | 0.51 | 30.1 |
| `DEVqmeyer` | 1.00 | 31.9 |
| `DEVfetlim` ×2 sites | 1.00 | 27.6 |
| `DEVpnjlim` ×2 sites | 1.00 | 19.0 |
| `DEVlimvds` ×2 sites | 1.00 | 9.7 |
| **total** | | **1,194.0** |

MOS1 for comparison: self 759.2, whole per-instance evaluation **1,068**
(`NIintegrate` 159.0, `exp` 68.8, `DEVqmeyer` 29.5, `DEVfetlim` 27.5,
`DEVpnjlim` 15.0, `DEVlimvds` 9.0).

### Where we are AHEAD

- **The `<dev>temp` preamble is free for us and is not free for ngspice.**
  `mos6load.c:122-147` computes `EffectiveLength`, the two `SatCur`s, three
  overlap capacitances, `betac` and `OxideCap` **every instance every
  iteration**, under its own apologetic comment ("these could be pre-computed,
  but for historical reasons are still done here"). ~8 flops and ~10 loads.
  Our `precompute` + `hp` prefix latch make it **0**. This is the one place the
  reference is structurally behind us, and it is real.
- **The convergence prediction and the bypass ladder.** `mos6load.c:243-303`
  builds `vgdo`, five `delv*`, `cdhat` and `cbhat` (~22 flops) and then runs six
  nested `fabs`/`MAX` compares, all of it *speculative* work to decide whether
  to skip the device. We do not have bypass and do not pay for it. Our
  convergence test is on the solver's `dx`.
- **End to end, decisively.** 0.908 / 0.946 with a device path that is ~1.24×
  ngspice's per instance. `CKTterr` alone is 18.6% of ngspice on pi100 and
  11.2% on mos6 against our `stepBound`'s 3.3%.

### Where we are BEHIND, in order of size

1. **The Jacobian. 488 Ir against roughly 20 flops.** ngspice writes
   `gm`, `gds`, `gmbs` as three hand-derived expressions folded into the same
   ~35-flop core it computes the current with (`mos6load.c:517-536`). We
   propagate 8 derivative lanes through 121 derivative-carrying S-ops. This is
   the entire structural gap and it is not fat — see §1. Reducing it means
   reducing the width, and k=6 is a measured 21% loss while k=4 is unsound.
2. **Limiting: `D.limit` 291 Ir against ngspice's 56.3.** ngspice's whole
   limiter ladder for mos6 is `DEVfetlim` 27.6 + `DEVpnjlim` 19.0 +
   `DEVlimvds` 9.7 = **56.3 Ir**, and it runs *inline inside* `MOS6load` on
   values already in registers. We walk the instances a second time and
   re-gather all `n_u` unknowns. (doc item 2, `wt-limit2`.)
3. **The stamp: 61 scattered RMWs at 195 Ir against ngspice's 26 at ~78.**
   ngspice's `MOS6DdPtr`-style pointers are resolved once at setup, so each
   stamp is one load, one add, one store. Ours goes through
   `slots[id·n_u² + ru·n_u + cu]` — a double indirection — and stamps 61 slots
   for 21 resistive plus 16 reactive live columns. (doc items 4/7,
   `wt-jacrows`.)
4. **The junction diode, deliberately.** `mos6.va:348-356` uses the
   tangent-continued exponent `isat·(exp(ux)·(1 + (v/vt − ux)) − 1)` where
   `mos6load.c:428-431` uses the plain `isat·(exp(min(v/vt, MAX_EXP_ARG)) − 1)`
   — about twice the flops, on purpose, because a flat clamp zeroes the
   restoring slope for LTE probes and sweep warm starts that evaluate at
   unlimited `x`. Not a defect; recorded so it is not "found" again.
5. **Model value arithmetic: 176 Ir against ~55 flops.** Partly the item above,
   partly that the Meyer ladder is written out in full where ngspice calls a
   shared `DEVqmeyer` (31.9 Ir).

Everything else — transcendental count, transcendental cost, the junction-charge
guard, the Meyer partition — is at parity.

## 3. Side results

- **doc item 6 is FIXED and can be closed.** `updateState` on mos6 now measures
  **330 Ir/call** (`urig.zig`, two-point fit) against the doc's 556 before
  542e9a0 — **−41%**, which is the two non-integer `pow` the shared `ln` removed.
  The `RealFor` charge-only pass measures **299 Ir/call**.
- **Lead 3 (`pcExpensive`'s libm-root filter) is CLOSED.** The filter no longer
  exists: `codegen.zig:833 pcConsider` now admits every non-folding instruction
  ("a libm-cost walk cannot change the answer"). Verified in the generated
  artifact — `grd`/`grs` are `hp[9]`/`hp[10]` and the four `czb*` are
  `hp[11..14]`, i.e. all six hoist today. The only pure values still recomputed
  per entry are `vcrits`/`vcritd`, and LLVM deletes them (§1).
- **Lead 2 (`sel`) has a smaller ceiling than estimated.** mos6 executes **2**
  `sel` per eval, worth 2 Ir, against 16 static sites; mos1 executes 7 of 19.
  The doc's −8.8 Ir is the mos6 ceiling and it is right. Static `S.sel` sites
  across all 38 devices: hisimhv 214, bsim4va 162, hisim2 120, bsimsoi 74,
  hicum 48, vdmos 24, bjt 22, mos1 19, bsim3 19, mos6 16. **If the eager-select
  machinery is ever built, price it on a whale, not on a gate deck** — the two
  CPU gate decks have nothing to give.
- **A contract divergence, noted and deliberately not fixed.** VerA's generated
  `R` (`codegen.zig:7738 rscalar_txt`) spells `abs`/`min`/`max`/`minC`/`maxC`
  as `@abs`/`@min`/`@max`, while `Dual` and `RealFor` use sign tests and
  comparison branches — they disagree at `-0` and on NaN (`engine.zig:344` says
  why). `R` also divides `a.v / b.v` where `Dual` uses `a.v * (1.0 / b.v)`.
  `engine.zig:2977` gates `RealFor` against `Dual` bit-for-bit; nothing gates
  `R` against `Dual`. **The `div` half of that is on purpose** — the generated
  `P` scalar's own comment states it: `R` is what `updateState` keeps, so
  changing it moves accepted-state bits. The `@abs`/`@min`/`@max` half looks
  unintended by the same standard, and "fixing" it would move those same bits
  for zero measured gain. Left alone; recorded so the next reader does not have
  to re-derive which half is deliberate.

## Settled by this pass — do not re-open without new evidence

| claim | the measurement that settled it |
|---|---|
| the AD layer has fat in it | per-call cost is at or **below** the naive AVX2 lowering for all 14 primitives; 306 attributed Ir against 488 nominal |
| gradient work on provably-zero derivatives | already deleted by LLVM inside the `always_inline` body — `con` and `lt` attribute **0 Ir** across 121 calls |
| gradient lanes no consumer reads | union of `jac_pattern` ∪ `q_pattern` is `0xff`; there is no dead lane |
| `vcrits`/`vcritd`'s two per-entry `ln` | dead-coded in all three callers: **−2 / 0 / 0 Ir** |
| `pcExpensive`'s libm-root filter | gone; `grd`/`grs`/`czb*` are `hp[9..14]` in the shipped artifact |
| `sel` on the CPU gate decks | 2 executed sites on mos6, 7 on mos1 — the −8.8 Ir ceiling stands |
| we are behind ngspice everywhere in the kernel | we are **ahead** on the `<dev>temp` preamble (0 vs ~15 Ir) and on the bypass/convergence-prediction ladder (0 vs ~40) |

## Where the next real win is, with its number

Not in `Dual`. In order of measured size:

1. **Limiting** — 291 against ngspice's **56.3**, and ngspice's is *inline*.
   234 Ir/instance/iterate. (`wt-limit2`)
2. **The stamp** — 195 against ngspice's ~78, with `q_pattern` saying 4 of 8
   charge rows are dead. 117 Ir/instance. (`wt-jacrows`)
3. **DONE — `docs/perf/rank4-2026-09-10.md`.** Landed as written below, with
   the partition in `ProtoStore.finalize` and a comptime `narrow` flag on
   `evalRange`; VerA emits the alias map as `collapse_full`. **pi100 −12.40%,
   mos6 −10.95%, 198 / 215 Ir per evaluation against the ≈230 bound this
   paragraph predicted, 256/256 raws byte-identical.** The original text stands:

   **Narrowing the derivative width is the only thing that touches the 488**,
   and the only sound route left is a *runtime-collapsed* universe: with
   `rd = rs = 0` — true on both gate decks — `di ≡ d` and `si ≡ s` and the two
   branch-flow unknowns are gone, so the true rank is 4, and `@Vector(4, f64)`
   is one ymm where `@Vector(8, f64)` is two. Upper bound ≈ 230 Ir/eval, 23% of
   the kernel. **It is not free and it is not small**: `Builder.addDevice`
   calls `D.collapse` per *instance* (`builder.zig:224`), so instances in one
   batch can disagree, and the scatter tapes are frozen at `n_u` across the GPU
   boundary. It needs a second comptime `S` width, a lane→column map, and an
   instance partition. Recorded with its number so the next pass can decide
   deliberately rather than rediscover k=6.

## Reproducing

```
/tmp/rig/measure.sh base mos6          # kernel Ir/eval, two-point fit, ~40 s
ABL=zero-all /tmp/rig/measure.sh z mos6   # the AD layer's total, by ablation
/tmp/rig/count-base                    # exact dynamic S-op census
RIG_MODE=update RIG_N=3000 /tmp/rig/urig  # updateState / RealFor passes

valgrind --tool=callgrind --cache-sim=no --branch-sim=no \
  --callgrind-out-file=/dev/null zig-out/bin/espice -b -r /tmp/o.raw \
  benchmark/fixtures/scaling/parallel_inverters_100/circuit.sp

# the reference, WITH symbols — the PATH ngspice is stripped and 8.5% slower
cd /tmp/ng/ngspice-44.2 && ./configure --enable-xspice --enable-cider \
  --disable-openmp --with-readline=no --prefix=/tmp/ng/inst CFLAGS="-g -O2" \
  && make -j8 && make install
# pi100 has no .print, so batch mode runs nothing without -r:
/tmp/ng/inst/bin/ngspice -b -r /tmp/o.raw <deck>
```
