# MOS1 AC on `sweep/opamp_wl_*` — DC, and neither model is wrong

`docs/perf/ac-source-2026-09-10.md` closed the excitation half of this
(`v(inp)` now reads `1+0j` in both engines) and left a factor of 2.8 on the
differential-pair drain: `v(d1_1)` ngspice **−2.0315** against espice
**−0.7319**, with a max absolute deviation of **3.1968e+05** across the deck.

**The fault is DC, not AC — and it is in neither engine's model.** The deck's
operating point is not determined at SPICE's default `abstol`. Every branch
current in it sits at **1.368e-12 A** against `abstol = 1e-12 A`, and the AC
gain of the node in question is a ratio of two gate overdrives of 30 µV and
113 µV. Both engines stop Newton inside their own convergence gates at
different points, and that is the whole 2.8x.

Asked to converge, the two engines agree to **1e-10** over all 15,008 signals
of `opamp_wl_5000`, and both reproduce an independent hand-solve of the 3x3
small-signal network to 6 digits. **No code changed.**

## 1. DC or AC — settled first

The deck declares no `TOX`, no `CGSO/CGDO/CGBO`, no `CJ/CJSW/CBD/CBS` and no
`AD/AS/PD/PS`. Both engines therefore carry **zero** MOS capacitance:

| | ngspice | espice |
|---|---|---|
| `Cox' = 0` when TOX not given | `mos1temp.c:61-62` (`if(!MOS1oxideThicknessGiven \|\| ==0) MOS1oxideCapFactor = 0`) | `mos1.va:133-134` (`if ($param_given(tox) && tox > 0.0) … else coxp = 0.0`) |
| junction charge skipped when `Cbs==Cbssw==0` | `mos1load.c:567,628` | `mos1.va:110` (`jchg`'s first guard) |

`ngspice -b` `show` on the deck confirms it: `cgs = cgd = cgb = cbd = cbs = 0`
on all five transistors. The only capacitor in each OTA instance is
`Cl = 100f` on `out_*`, and `d1_*` carries none at all. So at the low end of
`.ac dec 10 1 1G` the response at `d1_1` **is** the DC Jacobian solve, and the
capacitance partition cannot be the mechanism.

The operating points then disagree (single instance 1, reduced from the
fixture, both engines at deck defaults):

```
             v(d1_1)            v(tail_1)
ngspice      1.099862           0.1619496
espice       1.0992601997869926 0.16191271957085493
delta        -6.02e-4 V         -3.69e-5 V
```

## 2. Why 600 µV of DC is a factor of 2.8 of AC

`M5` has `vgs = VBIAS = 0.55` against `VTO = 0.7`: the tail source is in
cutoff, `gm = gds = gmbs = 0` (`mos1load.c:493-500`). The whole OTA is biased
off and every current in it is a gmin/saturation-current leak. At the
converged point, with ngspice's own numbers (`show`, `.options abstol=1e-18`):

```
             id            vgs        von        vdsat(=vgst)   gm           gds          gmb
M1_1   1.36791e-12   0.737987   0.737957   3.0072e-05    1.71601e-08  9.9476e-15   3.80863e-09
M3_1   1.36791e-12   0.700113  -0.700000   1.12743e-04   1.16689e-08  3.17773e-14   4.12496e-09
```

Two facts follow.

**(a) The gain is a ratio of overdrives.** For this topology
`v(d1)/v(inp) ≈ −gm1 / (2·gm3)` and ngspice computes
`gm = β(1 + λ·vds)·vgst` in saturation (`mos1load.c:505,508`), so the gain is
`−(β1/2β3)·(vgst1/vgst3)` — a ratio of a 30 µV and a 113 µV overdrive, each of
which is the difference of two ~0.74 V numbers.

**(b) `abstol` buys 86 µV of that 113 µV.** `dId/dV ≈ gm3 = 1.16689e-8 S`, so
a 1e-12 A slack in the node current is `1e-12 / 1.16689e-8 = 8.57e-5 V` of
freedom on `v(d1_1)` — **76 % of `vgst3`**. `gm3` inherits that 76 %, and the
gain with it.

The measured stopping points, recomputed from the published level-1 formulas
against the exact node voltages each engine printed (channel current
`mos1load.c:505-523` plus `cbd` from `mos1load.c:441`):

| OP | `cd(M1)` | `cd(M3)` | KCL residual at `d1_1` | `vgst3` | `gm3` | `−gm1/2gm3` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ngspice, default tol | 4.420927e-12 | 1.695672e-12 | **2.73e-12** (2.7·abstol) | 1.380e-4 | 1.4283e-8 | −2.1519 |
| espice, default tol | 7.769971e-12 | 2.903475e-11 | **2.13e-11** (21·abstol) | 7.398e-4 | 7.6572e-8 | −0.5693 |
| converged (both) | 1.367907e-12 | 1.367907e-12 | 2.8e-24 | 1.1274e-4 | 1.1669e-8 | **−0.7353** |

(The recomputed `cd` values match ngspice's printed `id` to its 6 printed
digits — 1.367907e-12 vs `1.36791e-12` — so the formula transcription is
verified, not assumed.)

**Neither default OP satisfies KCL to `abstol`.** That is not a bug in either
engine: both use *delta*-based convergence, not an absolute KCL gate.
ngspice's device test is
`tol = reltol·MAX(|cdhat|,|cd|) + abstol` on the change in drain current
(`mos1conv.c:79-81`), which on a device whose entire drain current is
1.368e-12 A is a **73 % gate**. espice's is the Δx norm
(`solvers/converger.zig:652-662`, ngspice's `CKTconvTest` form) plus a
diagonal-scaled residual, `tol = max(residual_tol, 10·|diag_i|·(reltol·|x_i| +
vntol))` (`solvers/converger.zig:288-290`), which at `|x| ≈ 1.1 V` is ~8e-10 A
here. Both gates are wide open on this deck.

| `gmbs = gm·GAMMA/(2·sarg)` | `mos1load.c:491,510` | AD of `von(vbs)` | identical |

| AC stamp = DC conductances + `ω·C` | `mos1acld.c:91-113` | `Circuit.linearize` → one `eval()` at `x_op`, `freq_solve.fromCircuit` | identical |
| Meyer caps in AC = `2·half + overlap` | `mos1acld.c:59-67` | `mos1.va:357-359` (`$prev` latched at OP exit) | identical |

`LAMBDA` was the named suspect ("a 2.8× on a drain node smells like output
conductance"). It is not: `gds1 = 9.9476e-15` and `gds3 = 3.17773e-14` at the
converged point, four orders of magnitude below `gm3 = 1.16689e-8`. Setting
`gds1 = 0` outright in the analytic network below moves `v(d1_1)` from
−2.0315 to −2.1478 at ngspice's own OP — it is not a 2.8x lever anywhere on
this deck.

The junction conductances are worth singling out because this deck is the
best possible test of them: `out_1` is loaded by `gds4 + gds2 + gbd4 + gbd2 +
jωCl` and nothing else — no `gm` reaches that node — so `gbd` is 92 % of its
2.2e-12 S admittance and its gain of ~2.6e4 is essentially `1/gbd`. Section 4
shows the two engines agreeing on that node to 6e-6 absolute out of 7.7e3
once the OP is shared, which pins `gbd` to ~1e-9 relative. Both are
`CKTgmin = 1e-12` in the reverse branch (`mos1load.c:432,440` against
`mos1.va:90`) and `Isat·e^{v/vt}/vt + gmin` forward
(`mos1load.c:436,444` against `mos1.va:89`); ngspice prints
`gbs(M5_1) = 1.38662e-12`, which is `1e-14/0.02585 + 1e-12` — the same
expression espice evaluates.

`gds`'s `Beta`-not-`betap` asymmetry at `mos1load.c:509` deserves a note
because it looks like a divergence and is not: `Id = ½·Beta·(1+λ·vds)·vgst²`,
so `∂Id/∂vds = ½·λ·Beta·vgst²` exactly. ngspice writes the analytic
derivative; espice differentiates the same expression. Same number.

## 5. Proof 2 — analytic hand-solve of the small-signal network

The 5T OTA at AC is three unknowns (`d1`, `out`, `tail`) with `inp = 1`,
`inn = vdd = vbias = 0`. Writing the ngspice stamp
(`mos1acld.c:91-113`, which for `xnrm = 1` is
`I_dp = gds(v_dp−v_sp) + gm(v_g−v_sp) + gmbs(v_b−v_sp) + gbd(v_dp−v_b)`) for
each of the five devices and solving by hand — `mos1-ac-2026-09-10-oracle.py`
beside this file, 40 lines, no simulator involved, and it asserts both rows
below:

| input | `v(d1_1)` from the hand-solve | simulator |
|---|---|---|
| ngspice's own **unconverged** OP parameters | −2.031531 − 0.033584j | ngspice default: −2.03154 − 0.03358j |
| the **converged** OP parameters | −0.7319449 − 0.0010068j | both engines: −0.7319427 − 0.0010067j |

Six digits both times, from ngspice's own 6-digit printed `gm`/`gds`/`gmbs`.
The −2.0315 is a faithful evaluation of a **wrong operating point**; −0.7319 is
the small-signal gain of this circuit.

(The output node is an extreme case in its own right: `out_1` is loaded only
by `gds4 + gds2 + gbd4 + gbd2 + jωCl` ≈ 2.2e-12 S — no `gm` reaches it — so its
gain is ~2.6e4 and it is even more sensitive than `d1_1`. That is why the
absolute deviation reads 3.1968e+05.)

## 6. Proof 3 — the model itself, at a bias that is not degenerate

The same OTA with `VBIAS = 1.1` (tail transistor actually on, `W/L` widened),
deck defaults, no tolerance games:

```
WORST max 1.2179e-12  rms 7.8638e-13   var=v(d1)   (11 vars compared)
```

And a deck that exercises the parts the fixture does not — Meyer capacitance
(`TOX=20n`), gate overlap (`CGSO/CGDO/CGBO`), junction capacitance
(`CJ/CJSW/MJ/MJSW/PB/FC`), lateral diffusion, `RD/RS/RSH`, body bias, a
linear-region device, a saturated device and a PMOS, over `.ac dec 20 1 10G`:

```
WORST max 2.4273e-07  rms 6.6294e-08   var=v(dc)
```

That 2.4e-07 does **not** improve with tolerance, and it is the one genuine
constant-level divergence found in this audit. Isolating `Cox` alone (TOX, no
overlap, no junction caps, extracted from an RC pole):

```
C_ngspice = 1.942388726714e-14   C_espice = 1.942382790835e-14   rel = -3.0560e-06
```

exactly `8.8541878128e-12 / 8.854214871e-12 − 1`. ngspice's SPICE3-lineage MOS
models hardcode the 1970s vacuum permittivity `8.854214871e-12`
(`mos1temp.c:64,90`; also `mos2temp.c:15,65,90`); espice's `.va` files use the
CODATA value `8.8541878128e-12` (10 model files). **Not changed here**: 3 ppm
is five orders of magnitude below the 1e-2/1e-3 gate, it is frequency- and
bias-independent, and re-keying the oxide permittivity in 10 models to a
superseded physical constant would move every TOX-bearing fixture for no
measurable gain. Recorded so the next audit does not rediscover it.

## 8. What is actually left

Nothing in the MOS1 model. Two things adjacent to it, both quantified above
and neither touched:

- **`sweep/opamp_wl_{200,1000,5000}` are not a valid accuracy reference as
  written.** They score `N/A` in the harness today (complex plot, unvalidated)
  so nothing is being gated on them, but the 3.1968e+05 in
  `docs/perf/ac-source-2026-09-10.md` is measuring `abstol` slack, not physics.
  The root-cause fix is on the deck: `VBIAS = 0.55` against `VTO = 0.7` puts
  every transistor in cutoff, which is not what a "5T OTA gain sweep" fixture
  is for. Raising `VBIAS` above `VTO` (or adding `.options abstol=1e-18`)
  turns it back into a test of the small-signal model. This is the same
  finding as `fail-audit-2026-09-10.md`'s closing section — a reference that
  has not converged — arrived at independently on an AC deck.
- **espice's residual gate is voltage-referred.** `converger.zig:288-290` uses
  `10·|diag_i|·(reltol·|x_i| + vntol)`, which on a 1.1 V node is ~8e-10 A
  against ngspice's `abstol`-referred 1e-12 A device-current gate
  (`mos1conv.c:79-81`). It costs nothing on the shipped fixtures — espice
  lands on the converged answer on all three `opamp_wl_*` decks — but it is
  ~800x looser on this deck class and it is why the one-instance reduction
  stops early. Changing it retunes Newton on every deck in the suite and is a
  separate, measurable step; it is not a MOS1 model question.
